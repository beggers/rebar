# Complete original-correctness campaign, version 2

This freeze fixes how a complete Python regular-expression compatibility test
is saved. It does not run or activate a candidate, compile code, measure
performance, open the holdout, or change the original tests.

CPython 3.14.6 remains the separately pinned baseline. The exact original
correctness test retains its 13 suites, 31,237 cases, 152 upstream public
records, one genuine debug-build skip, and the same 13 explicitly named
private-only waivers. All 25 separately owned first-party source files across
C, Rust, Zig, C++, Go, and Fortran are unchanged. There are still five
successful source builds, three previously runnable language families, and
169 real historical compiler processes.

The original version-1 campaign runner, version-2 suite producer, and
version-4 reversible activator are authenticated by their complete frozen
source, protocol, and machine hashes. An explicitly authorized future run
reuses the exact version-1 original-suite evaluator, worker isolation, full
standard-output and standard-error limits, failure interpretation, 13-suite
order, and restoration in `finally`. Only version-1's temporary in-memory
publication and fresh-name hooks are replaced. This freeze does not execute
that run.

The 65 original historical evidence owners and the two independently
authenticated version-1 C++ failure owners make 67 retained original
candidate-evidence owners. Two additional, separately authenticated Go
infrastructure-failure owners bring the actual current repository evidence
total to 69. These denominators are deliberately distinct. The C++ publication
receipt passed; its candidate failed. Its complete 13 suites, all 31,237
cases, verified restoration, original archive, separate receipt, and true
failure remain preserved.

The separately source-pinned Go failure preserver, its frozen protocol and
machine, and its owner-only failure archive and separate receipt are all
authenticated. The archive retains the complete bytes of five genuine private
activation owners. Its passing receipt certifies failure-evidence publication
only: original Go infrastructure remains **FAIL**, Go correctness remains
**NOT VERIFIED**, and the candidate does not qualify. Original Go suite
outcomes, mismatch counts, exact original report length, and restoration
route remain **NOT RECORDED**. Missing original Go success or failure
campaign archives are never fabricated.

Version 1 assembled the complete canonical report and rejected reports above
256 MiB. Version 2 passes `json.JSONEncoder.iterencode` fragments directly
through a deterministic, zero-mtime, single-member gzip stream. It preserves
sorted keys, ASCII escapes, nonfinite-number rejection, original lone Unicode
surrogates, and exactly one terminal newline. Both complete uncompressed JSON
and compressed evidence have independent strict four-GiB limits and
incremental SHA-256 hashes. Full original worker stdout and stderr are not
truncated. The limit is deliberately larger than 13 times the original
64-MiB stdout and 4-MiB stderr limits because canonical base64, decoded
observations, repeated mismatch records, and metadata add real overhead.
An arbitrary unbounded traceback is not claimed to fit: overflow fails
closed without publishing or qualifying a partial result.

An actual archive is created exclusively as a no-follow, owner-only 0600
inode. Its entire gzip stream is reread in bounded chunks through that same
authenticated inode; compressed and decompressed byte counts, both complete
SHA-256 hashes, the sole gzip member, and its zero timestamp are verified
before directory synchronization and before a separate exclusive 0600
receipt is published. Archive and receipt inodes must differ. Both files and
their directories are synchronized. Partial, swapped, truncated, oversized,
aliased, concatenated, or unrestored outputs are rejected. Only exact fresh
version-2 evidence names may be used.

`--self-test` is entirely synthetic and blocks files, processes, clocks,
threads, networking, temporary files, and candidate imports. It attacks
every frozen original suite, first-party owner, historical failure,
both real Go failure-evidence owners and all five original raw records,
publication policy, exact canonical chunk boundaries, missing chunks,
truncated or concatenated gzip members, false hashes, overflow, fabricated
receipts, aliasing, and false restoration. It proves the old 256-MiB
boundary can be crossed using integer controls without allocating that
report. `--verify-frozen-context` is read-only and authenticates the
unchanged original evaluator, producer, activator, all inherited historical
evidence, and both real C++ failure owners. Neither verification starts a
candidate or writes a file.

There are zero newly correctness-qualified candidates. Performance, memory,
speedups, confidence intervals, rankings, and regressions remain **NOT
MEASURED**. The holdout is **NOT OPENED**. No winner is selected.
